Dynamometer Wattmeter: Single-Phase Power Measurement

Wire the wattmeter, choose its ranges, switch on the supply and measure the power of an R–L load. Compare the reading with the true power.

Theory

A fixed current coil (CC) carries the load current. A moving pressure coil (PC), in series with a high resistance, carries a current proportional to the voltage. The mean torque is ∝ V I cos φ, so the scale is uniform and the pointer reads power.
Reading = deflection × multiplier, multiplier = Vrated Irated cos φrated / full-scale divisions (150).
PC on load side: W reads load power + V²/Rp (pressure coil loss).
PC on supply side: W reads load power + I²Rc (current coil loss).
The PC has a small inductance, so its current lags V by a small angle β. This causes a reading error of about β·tan φ, which is large at low power factor. Use an LPF wattmeter when cos φ is low.

Procedure

  1. Set the variac to 0 V. Choose ranges above the expected V and I, and the UPF type for a resistive load.
  2. Switch the MCB on and raise the variac slowly to about 200 V. Note V, I and the deflection.
  3. Record the reading. Compare W observed with I²R and with VI.
  4. Add inductance L. The current falls and cos φ falls. Switch to the LPF type and note the larger deflection.
  5. Change the PC connection and observe which loss is added to the reading.
  6. If the pointer deflects backwards, press Reverse PC terminals. Never exceed the current or voltage range.
