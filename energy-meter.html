Calibration and Testing of a Single Phase Energy Meter

Wire the circuit, load the meter with lamps, count its impulses and find its percentage error. The dotted banner details can be edited.

Aim

To calibrate and test a single phase static energy meter by direct loading, and to find its percentage error.

Apparatus

  • 1-phase 230 V, 50 Hz supply with MCB
  • Voltmeter 0–400 V, ammeter 0–8 A, wattmeter 0–1200 W
  • Static energy meter, 3200 imp/kWh
  • Lamp load, ten lamps of 100 W each, and a stopwatch

Procedure

  1. Make the connections by clicking one terminal and then another. Click a wire to remove it. Wrong wires can be cleared with Clear wires.
  2. Connections: S1–A1, A2–V1, V1–Mw, Mw–C, S2–V2, S2–V, V–N1, L–M, N2–L2, Le–L1.
  3. Press Check connections. When they are correct, click the MCB to turn the supply on.
  4. Select the number of lamps from the Lamp load list.
  5. Press Start on the stopwatch and count the blinks of the green LED on the energy meter.
  6. Press Stop when the stopwatch reads 00:01:00. Use 5× or 10× speed to shorten the wait.
  7. Enter the blink count and press Add reading. The reading goes into the observation table.
  8. Repeat with different numbers of lamps, then use the other tabs for the theory calculation, graph and model calculation.
  9. Press Print to print the page, or Reset experiment to start again.

Energy measurement

Actual energy A = V × I × T / 1000 kWh (T in hours)
Recorded energy R = N / K kWh, N = impulses, K = 3200 imp/kWh
Relative error = (R − A) / A × 100 %

Induction type (rotating disc) energy meter

The disc speed follows the load.

Construction

  • Driving system: a shunt magnet with a many-turn pressure coil across the supply, and a series magnet with a few-turn current coil in the load circuit.
  • Moving system: a light aluminium disc on a spindle, running in the air gap of both magnets.
  • Braking system: a permanent magnet near the disc edge, with an adjustable position.
  • Registering system: a worm and gear train that turns the dials as the spindle rotates.

Working

  • The current coil makes flux φ1 proportional to I. The pressure coil makes flux φ2 proportional to V, lagging it by about 90° (set by the copper shading bands).
  • The two alternating fluxes induce eddy currents in the disc. Their interaction gives a driving torque Td ∝ φ1 φ2 sin α, which equals V I cos φ, the power.
  • The braking torque is proportional to speed, Tb ∝ N. At steady speed Td = Tb, so N ∝ P.
  • Revolutions in a time t ∝ P × t, the energy. Energy = revolutions ÷ meter constant (rev/kWh).
  • Calibration: brake magnet position (speed), lag adjustment (phase) and friction compensation (light load).
