3. Theory

A capacitor with dielectric loss is represented by a perfect capacitor Cx in series with a small resistance rx. It forms one arm. The opposite arm is a loss-free standard capacitor C2. The third arm is a non-inductive resistor R3, and the fourth is a resistor R4 in parallel with a variable capacitor C4. At balance Z1Z4 = Z2Z3, and equating real and imaginary parts gives:

Cx = C2 R4 / R3
rx = R3 C4 / C2
tan δ = ω rx Cx = ω C4 R4

R4 sets the capacitance balance and C4 sets the loss balance. The tan δ equation does not contain R3 or C2, so the dissipation factor is read directly from C4 and R4 at a known frequency. High-voltage Schering bridges apply several kilovolts to test insulation under working stress.

4. Procedure

  1. Select R3. The balance value of R4 is Cx·R3/C2, so a larger R3 needs a larger R4.
  2. Adjust R4 with the slider and the step buttons until the detector output falls to a minimum.
  3. Adjust C4 to reduce the output further. Repeat R4 and C4 in turn until the status reads Balanced.
  4. Press Record reading. Cx and tan δ are calculated and compared with the actual values.
  5. Press New unknown capacitor and repeat with another R3. If no balance is possible, change R3.
